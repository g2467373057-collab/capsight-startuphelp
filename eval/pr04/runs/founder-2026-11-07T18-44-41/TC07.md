# TC07 · Restaurant reservations: market research report

> Test run, In-vitro (fictional founder idea). Generated 2026-11-07T18:59:49.382Z. Evidence only; the founder decides.

**Founder prompt:** I want to build subscription software for independent restaurants in Singapore to manage reservations, waitlists and reminders. Please research market, rules, grants, competitors and their official prices. If a price is not public, say so.

**Read as:** Restaurant reservation and waitlist software · customer: Independent restaurants · market: Singapore

## Key takeaways

This looks like a market served by strong international platforms rather than a proven local crowd: Hostme, Resy, Toast Tables and Yelp Guest Manager all ship reservation and waitlist functionality [^6][^8][^10][^13], but the evidence names no Singapore-based rival, so local density is unconfirmed. The regulatory load is real but bounded, centred on data protection obligations that apply from the moment overseas-origin personal data is collected [^2] and extend to how hosting vendors are secured [^3]. Support exists on paper through the EDGE Grant's Automation & Digitalisation area [^5], though the PSG that offered up to 50% of eligible costs up to $30,000 has closed [^4] and EDGE's actual terms for software are not in the evidence. Pricing room is the clearest opening: published plans run from about $98 to $459 per month [^16][^17][^21], with Toast Tables' price unpublished [^19], leaving space at the low end for independent restaurants. The biggest opportunity is that price gap for a localised product; the biggest risk is that the incumbents bundle consumer booking demand that a new entrant cannot match [^8][^9]. First to validate is whether independent Singapore restaurants will pay for reservations and waitlists as standalone software, since the evidence gives visitor growth [^1] but no local restaurant count or software spend figure.

## 1. Market size and growth

The only demand-side evidence here is about Singapore's accommodation sector, which grew 6.6 per cent year-on-year in the fourth quarter of 2025 and 3.1 per cent for the full year, with about 4.0 million visitors in the quarter, up 2.5 per cent [^1]. That points to a visitor base that is still expanding, which is the pool that feeds restaurant covers and therefore reservation and waitlist volume. It does not, however, measure restaurants themselves: public sources found so far do not give a count of independent restaurants in Singapore or a spend figure for reservation software, so the addressable market for this specific product cannot be sized from the evidence. The growth signal is directional and supportive, but it is a proxy for demand rather than a direct measure of it.

## 2. Policy and regulation

The regulatory load that matters most here is data protection, because a reservation and waitlist product holds guest names, contact details and visit history. An organisation in Singapore collecting personal data originating from outside Singapore for its own purposes in Singapore must comply with all Data Protection Provisions from the time it seeks to collect the data or brings it into Singapore [^2]. The PDPC's 2021 guide also lists requiring the IT vendor providing website hosting to securely configure and adequately protect servers and networks against unauthorised access [^3]. For this idea that means the vendor relationship is itself a compliance topic, not just an engineering choice, and restaurants will likely expect the software to carry that burden for them. The evidence covers the obligations but not any licensing or sector-specific approval for restaurant booking software, and public sources found so far do not indicate one.

## 3. Government grants and support

The grant landscape shifted recently: the Productivity Solutions Grant ceased on 29 September 2026, and from 30 September onwards businesses apply for support under the EDGE Grant [^4]. Under the old scheme, PSG offered up to 50% of eligible costs for local SMEs, with up to $30,000 in support, and required at least 30% local equity held directly or indirectly by Singaporeans and/or Singapore PRs [^4]. The EDGE Grant, issued by Enterprise Singapore, covers 8 business areas and over 150 activities, including Automation & Digitalisation [^5]. That last point is the relevant hook, since reservation and waitlist software sits naturally under digitalisation, but the evidence does not state EDGE funding percentages, caps or whether subscription software specifically qualifies. Public sources found so far do not give those terms, which means the actual subsidy a restaurant could claim toward this product is unknown.

## 4. Competitive landscape

The competitive set named in the evidence is entirely international, not local: Hostme is a Washington, DC-based hospitality technology company offering an all-in-one cloud restaurant reservation, table and waitlist management platform for restaurants, bars, wineries and chains [^6][^7]. Resy is a restaurant reservation and table-management platform founded in 2014, with a consumer booking app and Resy OS software for restaurants, owned by American Express [^8][^9]. Toast Tables is Toast's restaurant reservation, waitlist and table-management software, connecting guest bookings, host workflows and floor plans [^10][^11][^12], and Yelp Guest Manager combines reservations, waitlists, table and floor-plan management, and takeout order management [^13][^14][^15]. These are well-capitalised players with consumer-facing demand engines attached, which is the hard part to replicate. The evidence does not name any Singapore-based competitor or any localisation of these products for the Singapore market, so whether the field is crowded locally is not established.

## 5. Competitor pricing

Published prices sit at the premium end: Hostme lists Mezzo at $109/mo ($98 annual), Grande at $169/mo ($152 annual), and Enterprise custom with contact required for 10+ units [^16][^7]. Resy is $289/month for Platform and $459/month for Platform 360 [^17][^18]. Yelp Guest Manager is $249/month for Basic and $399/month for Plus [^21][^15][^22]. Toast Tables is the outlier in transparency: its base plan includes unlimited waitlist and 25 reservations per month, and Toast Tables Plus includes unlimited reservations, but no dollar figure is given [^19][^20][^12]. Public sources found so far do not give a price for Toast Tables or for Hostme Enterprise, so the true low end of the market is unpriced in the evidence. The visible range of roughly $98 to $459 per month leaves obvious room beneath it, though none of these prices are stated in Singapore dollars or confirmed as available in Singapore.
