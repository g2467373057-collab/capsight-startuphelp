# TC06 · Minimart inventory: market research report

> Test run, In-vitro (fictional founder idea). Generated 2026-11-07T18:56:55.507Z. Evidence only; the founder decides.

**Founder prompt:** I want to build inventory software for independent minimarts in Singapore to track stock, expiry dates and reordering. Please research market, rules, grants, at least three relevant competitors and their prices. If fewer than three can be verified, explain the gap.

**Read as:** Inventory software for minimarts · customer: Independent minimarts · market: Singapore

## Key takeaways

This looks like a crowded general inventory-software market with a thin, specific opening at the low-price end for shops that care about expiry dates and simple reordering. The regulatory load is light — mainly PDPA data handling [^3], with the PDPC's own guidance nudging businesses toward keeping data inventories [^4] — and grant support exists but has just been reshuffled into the EDGE Grant, where digital transformation is assessed case by case [^5][^6][^7]. Pricing room is clearest below Zoho's S$39 entry tier [^15][^16][^17], since the other relevant competitors' prices are not publicly verifiable. The biggest opportunity is that none of the verified competitors is described as serving minimarts or expiry tracking [^9][^10][^11][^12][^13][^14]; the biggest risk is that the segment's size and willingness to pay are unproven, since no source gives a minimart count or segment value. The first thing to validate is how many independent minimarts exist and what they currently pay for stock control, followed by whether EDGE support applies to a purchase at their scale.

## 1. Market size and growth

The closest official signal for this idea is trade activity rather than minimart software spend: Singapore's wholesale trade sector grew 6.1 per cent across 2025, after 5.6 per cent in 2024, and accelerated to 9.9 per cent year-on-year in the fourth quarter of 2025 [^1]. That tells us the distribution layer these shops buy from is expanding, which is a supportive backdrop for stock-tracking tools, but it is not a measure of demand for minimart software itself. The digital side is also growing, with the information and communications sector's real value added up 6.1% in 2025 against 5.0% GDP growth, and its GDP share rising from 5.9% to 6.3% [^2]. Public sources found so far do not give a count of independent minimarts in Singapore or a dollar size for this software segment, so the addressable market still has to be sized from the ground up.

## 2. Policy and regulation

The regulatory load here looks light and mostly indirect. The Personal Data Protection Act 2012 is the general law governing how organisations collect, use and disclose personal data [^3], which matters if the product stores supplier contacts, staff records or loyalty data. Notably, the PDPC's 2026 guidance recommends keeping an inventory of personal data, and says this can be done with spreadsheets or asset inventory software [^4] — a mild tailwind, since it makes structured record-keeping a recognised compliance habit. Nothing in the evidence points to sector-specific licensing or food-safety rules that would apply to inventory software for minimarts, so the main obligation is sensible data handling rather than a heavy compliance programme.

## 3. Government grants and support

The grant picture changed recently and that matters for how a minimart would pay for this. The Productivity Solutions Grant previously covered up to 50% of eligible costs for local SMEs, capped at $30,000 [^5], but PSG, EDG and MRA ceased on 29 September 2026, with applications from 30 September moving to the EDGE Grant [^5]. The EDGE Grant, run by Enterprise Singapore, spans 8 business areas and over 150 activities including Automation & Digitalisation [^6], which is the bucket this product would sit in. The catch is that large-scale digital transformation under EDGE is assessed case by case [^7], so a small minimart purchase may not get the same automatic support the old PSG gave — worth confirming with the scheme before quoting subsidy-inclusive pricing.

## 4. Competitive landscape

The named competitors are mostly general inventory platforms rather than minimart specialists. Cin7 is cloud inventory management and small business ERP connecting sales channels, suppliers and warehouses for real-time stock visibility, aimed at product sellers and wholesalers [^9][^10]; Zoho Inventory targets small and growing businesses tracking stock, orders, warehouses and fulfilment [^11][^12]; and Unleashed serves manufacturers, wholesalers and distributors with real-time stock, purchasing, production and reporting [^13][^14]. None of the evidence describes expiry-date tracking or the reorder rhythms of a convenience shop, which is the specific job this idea addresses. One listed competitor, WhiteBox, is a technical interview prep platform [^8] and is not a real comparable, so the verified relevant set is three, not four.

## 5. Competitor pricing

Only two of the four competitors have official prices in the evidence, and one of those is not a genuine comparable. WhiteBox lists Free $0/mo, Ultra Premium $21/mo (27% off from $29/mo) and DSA Premium with Mindsolve at $9/mo or $79/yr [^8], but as an interview prep tool it says nothing about inventory software pricing. Pricing for Cin7 and Unleashed is not publicly available in the sources found, so the mid and upper range for this category cannot be verified from evidence.

## What to validate next yourself

- Whether target customers really have this pain (needs customer interviews)
- Whether customers will pay your price (needs customer or transaction evidence)
- Check whether minimarts qualify for EDGE Grant funding for inventory software under Automation & Digitalisation.
- Assess competitor pricing and features, e.g., Zoho Inventory's S$39–S$329 per month tiers, against minimart needs.
- Verify if minimarts require personal data inventory features under PDPC guidelines and how that affects software design.
