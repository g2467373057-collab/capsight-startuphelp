# TC02 · AI assistant for businesses: market research report

> Test run, In-vitro (fictional founder idea). Generated 2026-11-07T18:48:03.415Z. Evidence only; the founder decides.

**Founder prompt:** I want to launch an AI assistant to help businesses save time in Singapore, but I have not decided which type of business or tasks to focus on. Please research the market, regulations, grants, competitors and prices.

**Read as:** AI assistant for businesses · customer: businesses · market: Singapore

## Key takeaways

This is a crowded market with room at the low-price end and in narrow, task-specific niches: general assistants like ChatGPT and Claude anchor the horizontal space [^9][^10][^11][^12][^13], while Slite Agent and Sintra AI show specialised positioning at $10–$20 per user/month and aggressive annual discounts [^14][^15][^16][^17][^23][^24][^25][^26]. The regulatory load is not uniform but is real for finance, healthcare and legal, where sector rules and MAS's FEAT Principles apply [^4], so the choice of target business type directly sets the compliance burden. Support exists through the EDGE Grant covering 8 business areas and over 150 activities [^6], plus the Investment Allowance [^7] and Startup SG Equity for deep tech [^8], though eligibility for a generic assistant is unconfirmed. The biggest opportunity is the fast-growing SME segment, where adoption tripled to 14.5% and future intent is rising [^2][^3]; the biggest risk is being undifferentiated against free and low-cost general tools. The first thing to validate is which specific business type and task set gives a defensible wedge, and whether that choice keeps the product inside or outside regulated sectors [^4] and eligible under EDGE activities [^6].

## 1. Market size and growth

AI adoption among Singapore enterprises is rising quickly: the overall rate climbed 8.8 percentage points from 2024 to 2025 to reach 23.5% [^1], and among firms with at least 10 employees it rose 13.2 points to 34.6% [^1]. The pipeline of future demand is also thickening, with 12.1% of non-adopting firms in 2025 planning to use AI within 12 months, up from 5.6% in 2023 [^2]. The sharpest movement is at the small-business end, where adoption tripled from 4.2% to 14.5% between 2023 and 2024, while larger enterprises moved from 44.0% to 62.5% [^3]. For an undecided AI assistant, this means the market is expanding fastest among SMEs that are still early in adoption, so the choice of business type matters more than the overall demand level.

## 2. Policy and regulation

The regulatory picture is sector-dependent rather than uniform: finance, healthcare and legal may be governed by existing sector-specific laws, regulations or guidelines [^4]. In financial services specifically, the Monetary Authority of Singapore has published the FEAT Principles to guide firms using AI and data analytics for financial products and services [^4]. Public sources found so far do not give a general AI-specific licensing or approval regime applying to all business types, which means the compliance burden will depend heavily on which sector and tasks the assistant targets. A horizontal assistant sold across sectors would inherit the strictest requirements of any regulated customer it serves.

## 3. Government grants and support

The grant landscape has recently shifted: the Enterprise Development Grant ceased on 29 September 2026, and from 30 September onwards businesses apply under the EDGE Grant [^5]. The EDGE Grant, issued by Enterprise Singapore, covers 8 business areas and over 150 activities [^6], which suggests a broad set of eligible activities an AI adoption project could potentially fall under. Separately, the Investment Allowance caps approved capital expenditure at S$10 million per project and was extended until 31 March 2026 [^7], and Startup SG Equity supports Singapore-based deep tech startups through co-investment alongside independent qualified third-party investors [^8]. Public sources found so far do not specify whether a generic business AI assistant qualifies under EDGE or Startup SG Equity, so the founder would need to confirm eligibility against the activity lists.

## 4. Competitive landscape

The competitive field is crowded and layered. At the general-purpose end, ChatGPT is a conversational assistant for answering questions, writing, creating images, coding and completing work, offered free or via paid plans for individuals and businesses [^9][^10], while Claude is an AI assistant and family of large language models used for writing, Q&A, coding and agentic work by individuals, teams and enterprises [^11][^12][^13]. More specialised players occupy adjacent niches: Slite Agent is an AI knowledge base for teams that drafts and maintains documentation and answers questions with cited sources [^14][^15], and Sintra AI offers pre-built AI employees automating socials, inbox, content, support and sales for small business owners [^16][^17]. For an undecided entrant, this means the horizontal space is dominated by well-resourced general assistants, and the more defensible openings look like specific tasks or workflows where a focused product can differentiate.

## 5. Competitor pricing

Published pricing shows a clear ladder from free to premium. ChatGPT runs Free $0, Plus $20, Pro from $100 (with Pro tiers at $100, $200 and $500), Business from $20 per user/month annual ($25 monthly), and custom Enterprise pricing [^18][^19][^20]. Claude offers Free $0, Pro at $17 per month annual or $20 monthly, Max $100, Ultra $200, Team seats from $20/seat/month annual, and custom Enterprise pricing [^11][^21][^22]. Slite Agent sits at Basic $10 and Pro $20 per user/month with custom Enterprise pricing [^23][^14][^24], while Sintra AI discounts heavily by commitment: $48.50/month on a 1-month plan (50% off), $23.60/month on 3 months (60% off) and $15.60/month on 12 months (70% off) [^25][^26]. This suggests the low end around $10–$20 per user/month is well populated, and pricing room may exist either below that for narrow task automation or above it for deeper, sector-specific outcomes.
