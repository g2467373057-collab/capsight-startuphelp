# TC05 · HR compliance tracker: market research report

**Founder prompt:** I want to build HR compliance software for small Singapore employers, with staff records and reminders. Different online articles seem to disagree about employer obligations. **Read as:** HR compliance software · customer: small Singapore employers · market: Singapore

## Key takeaways

This is a crowded market: four established, Singapore-localised HR and payroll platforms already cover staff records, leave, payroll and increasingly AI [^8][^9][^10][^12], so a records-and-reminders product enters as a late entrant unless compliance is the sharp edge. The regulatory load is moderate and concrete rather than sprawling, centred on PDPA retention and cross-border transfer duties [^4][^3], with the intermediary distinction [^5] shaping how the vendor itself must operate. Support exists but has shifted: PSG's 50% up to $30,000 [^6] is gone, replaced by the EDGE Grant's 8 areas and 150-plus activities [^7], and whether compliance software qualifies is unconfirmed. Pricing room is thin at the visible anchors of roughly US$1–3 per employee/month [^15] and S$4.25 per user/month [^16], with JustLogin's quote-only tiers [^17] the clearest gap for transparent compliance-led pricing. The biggest opportunity is encoding retention and transfer rules that generic HR suites treat lightly; the biggest risk is that buyers see compliance as a feature, not a purchase, and the segment size is still unquantified. What to validate first is whether small employers will pay separately for compliance depth, and whether EDGE covers this category.

## 1. Market size and growth

The only market-size evidence here is about Singapore's digital economy as a whole, not about HR software or compliance tooling for small employers. Digital economy value-added reached S$144.1 billion in 2025, 19.3% of GDP, and grew 5.6% year-on-year against nominal GDP growth of 3.1% [^1]. Enterprise AI adoption rose from 14.7% to 23.5% in 2025, and the tech workforce grew 3.8% to 222,200 [^2]. These point to a market that is digitising and adopting software faster than the overall economy, which is a favourable backdrop for a software product sold to local businesses. Public sources found so far do not give a size or growth rate for the small-employer HR compliance segment itself, so the specific addressable market for this idea remains unquantified.

## 2. Policy and regulation

The regulatory load is real but narrower than the conflicting articles suggest, and the original sources point to data protection as the core obligation set. The PDPA 2012 applies to collection, use and disclosure of personal data in Singapore unless expressly excluded [^3]. Two specific duties matter most for a records-and-reminders product: section 25 requires organisations to stop retaining personal data, or remove the means of association, once retention no longer serves its purpose and is no longer necessary for legal or business purposes [^4], and section 26 restricts transfers of personal data outside Singapore except as prescribed [^4]. A further nuance shapes the product model: a data intermediary processing personal data under a written contract is subject only to the Protection, Retention Limitation and Data Breach Notification Obligations, not the other Data Protection Provisions [^5]. For this idea, that means retention timers, deletion prompts and cross-border transfer checks are the rules the software can most directly encode, while the intermediary distinction affects how the vendor itself is regulated when handling customer employee data.

## 3. Government grants and support

The grant landscape has just been reset, which matters for how a small employer could fund adoption. PSG previously covered up to 50% of eligible costs for local SMEs, with up to $30,000 in support [^6], and eligibility required at least 30% local equity held by Singaporeans and/or PRs by ultimate individual ownership [^6]. PSG, EDG and MRA ceased on 29 September 2026, and from 30 September businesses apply under the EDGE Grant [^6], which Enterprise Singapore frames as covering 8 business areas and over 150 activities [^7]. The practical reading is that support still exists but the scheme a customer would use has changed, so any pricing or subsidy conversation with a small employer now runs through EDGE rather than PSG. The evidence does not say whether HR or compliance software specifically qualifies under EDGE, so that fit is unconfirmed.

## 4. Competitive landscape

This is a crowded field of established, Singapore-localised HR and payroll platforms. Talenox is a cloud multi-entity HRIS covering payroll, leave, employee profiles and expense claims, localised for Singapore, Malaysia and Hong Kong with a global plan [^8]. Swingvy is cloud HR and payroll software for SMEs covering HR hub, leave, time and attendance, claims, payroll and mobile across Singapore, Malaysia and Taiwan [^9]. JustLogin is a Singapore AI-powered HRMS SaaS covering payroll, leave, attendance, expense, benefits, appraisal, recruitment and an AI HR chatbot, aimed at SMEs across Southeast Asia [^10][^11], and QuickHR from Enable Business covers payroll, leave, claims, attendance, appraisals and AI for SMEs and enterprises [^12][^13][^14]. All four already hold the staff-records ground this idea would occupy, so differentiation would have to come from compliance depth rather than from records and reminders alone.

## 5. Competitor pricing

Published pricing shows a clear per-employee, low-ticket norm, with room at the entry level. Talenox prices payroll at US$1 per employee/month (from US$5 for the first five), leave at US$1 per employee/month (from US$5), and a suite at US$3 per employee/month (from US$15), with Talenox Pro custom [^15][^8]. Swingvy charges S$4.25 per user/month annually or S$5.00 monthly, with payroll add-ons at S$4.25/S$5.00 and time add-ons at S$2.55/S$3.00, and enterprise on request [^16]. JustLogin shows Essential, Growth and Enterprise tiers all as get-a-quote, with no prices on its pricing page [^11][^17][^18]. The visible anchors sit at a few dollars per employee per month, which caps what a standalone compliance add-on can charge unless it is bundled; JustLogin's opacity leaves an opening for transparent compliance-led pricing.
