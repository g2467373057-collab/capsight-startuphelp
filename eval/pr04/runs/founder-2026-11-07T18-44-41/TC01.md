# TC01 · Tuition centre scheduling: market research report

> Test run, In-vitro (fictional founder idea). Generated 2026-11-07T18:46:21.259Z. Evidence only; the founder decides.

**Founder prompt:** I want to build subscription software for small tuition centres in Singapore to manage student bookings, attendance, fee reminders and parent messages. Please research the market, regulations, government grants, competitors and their prices. Tell me what I still need to check.

**Read as:** Tuition centre management software · customer: Small tuition centres · market: Singapore

## Key takeaways

This is a crowded market with room at the low-price end: international tools like TutorBird and Teachworks already cover scheduling, billing and payroll [^9][^10][^11], Class Manager gives the core away free and monetises payments [^18][^19][^12], and Edulabs already targets Singapore tuition and enrichment centres directly [^13][^14]. The regulatory load is real but bounded — PDPA compliance, actively enforced by the PDPC, applies to the student and parent data at the heart of the product [^3], with an extra cross-border dimension if data comes from outside Singapore [^4]. Support exists but is in flux: PSG's up-to-50%-of-cost model has closed and been replaced by the broader EDGE Grant [^6][^5][^7], and Startup SG Equity is aimed at deep tech rather than this kind of software [^8]. Pricing room appears to sit between free and Edulabs' S$250–S$550 per month [^20], with per-tutor and per-lesson models above and below [^15][^17]. The biggest opportunity is a locally tuned, affordable product for small centres; the biggest risk is that free and incumbent tools already cover the core workflow, and the first things to validate are the actual number and software spend of small Singapore tuition centres, what they pay today, and whether EDGE supports their purchase.

## 1. Market size and growth

The only sector-level growth figure in the evidence is for accommodation, which grew 6.6 per cent year-on-year in the fourth quarter of 2025 and 3.1 per cent for the whole year, easing from 5.0 per cent in 2024 [^1]. That tells us nothing about tuition centres, so it cannot be used to size this market. The closest relevant data point is the Education sector, which had a nominal value added share of 25.2 per cent and real growth of 1.6 per cent in 2025 [^2] — a broad sector figure that says education is a large part of the economy but is growing slowly, and it does not isolate tuition or enrichment centres. Public sources found so far do not give a count of small tuition centres in Singapore, their software spend, or growth in this specific segment, which means the addressable market and its direction still need to be established before any sizing claim can be made.

## 2. Policy and regulation

The main regulatory frame is the PDPA, first enacted in 2012 and revised in 2020 [^3], and it is actively administered and enforced by the Personal Data Protection Commission, which also promotes data protection awareness [^3]. For a product holding student records, attendance and parent contact details, that means data protection obligations sit at the core of the offering rather than at the edge. The cross-border rule adds a further layer: an organisation collecting personal data originating from outside Singapore for its own purposes in Singapore must comply with all Data Protection Provisions from the time it seeks to collect the data or brings it into Singapore [^4]. The evidence does not detail specific PDPA obligations, consent requirements or penalties, so the exact compliance checklist for this product still needs to be confirmed.

## 3. Government grants and support

The grant landscape has just been reorganised: EDG, MRA and PSG ceased on 29 September 2026, and from 30 September onwards businesses apply under the EDGE Grant [^6]. EDGE is issued by Enterprise Singapore and covers 8 business areas and over 150 activities [^5], so it is the current scheme to look at rather than the older ones. The Productivity Solutions Grant, now closed, offered up to 50% of eligible costs for local SMEs up to $30,000 [^7], which indicates the scale of support that was available for adoption of productivity software — but the evidence does not say whether EDGE carries comparable rates or caps for this kind of solution. Startup SG Equity is issued by Enterprise Singapore and catalyses private sector investments for Singapore-based deep tech startups [^8], which does not match a subscription software business for tuition centres. Public sources found so far do not show whether a tuition centre buying this software, or the founder building it, would qualify under EDGE, so eligibility needs to be checked directly.

## 4. Competitive landscape

The competitive set is already populated across two fronts. On the international side, TutorBird covers student management, scheduling, billing, online tutoring and a website builder for tutors, tutoring centres and test prep centres [^9], while Teachworks automates scheduling, billing, invoicing and tutor payroll for tutoring and test prep businesses as well as music, language and driving schools [^10][^11]. Class Manager takes a different route as free class management SaaS for dance studios, gymnastics clubs and similar activity businesses, automating billing, scheduling, enrolment, attendance and family communications [^12]. Most directly, Edulabs is a cloud-based school management system for tuition and enrichment centres in Singapore, automating registration, scheduling, invoicing, communication and reporting [^13][^14] — meaning the exact customer and geography are already served. The functional overlap with the proposed booking, attendance, fee reminder and parent messaging scope is high, and the evidence does not show how deeply any of these handle parent messaging or local payment habits.

## 5. Competitor pricing

Pricing spans a very wide range. TutorBird charges a single plan of $16.95/month for one tutor plus $4.95 per additional tutor or staff member [^15][^16][^9], which scales with headcount. Teachworks uses tiers of $16.49/mo plus $0.350 per student lesson, $47.99/mo plus $0.210 per student lesson, and $187.99/mo plus $0.080 per student lesson [^17], so cost rises with lesson volume. Class Manager is free with no monthly subscription, taking only a flat per-transaction fee on online payments (3.9% + 30¢) and tailored pricing for high volumes [^18][^19][^12], which anchors the low end at zero subscription. Edulabs sits at the top with S$250 to S$550 per month billed annually, plus a one-time software license fee quoted after a demo [^20][^13][^14]. For a small tuition centre, that leaves a clear gap between free and S$250+ per month, but the evidence does not show what small centres in Singapore actually pay today or how price-sensitive they are.

## What to validate next yourself

- Whether target customers really have this pain (needs customer interviews)
- Whether customers will pay your price (needs customer or transaction evidence)
- Check whether the EDGE Grant covers software adoption for tuition centres and what funding amount is available.
- Assess how many small tuition centres in Singapore currently use pen-and-paper or spreadsheets versus existing software.
- Review PDPA requirements for storing student and parent data to estimate compliance effort and cost.
