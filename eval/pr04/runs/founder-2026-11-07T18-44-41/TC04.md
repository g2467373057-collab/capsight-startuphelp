# TC04 · SME grant finder: market research report

> Test run, In-vitro (fictional founder idea). Generated 2026-11-07T18:51:32.638Z. Evidence only; the founder decides.

**Founder prompt:** I want to build software to help Singapore SMEs find digitalisation grants. I heard new applications for EDG and PSG are still open. Please check this against current official sources and dates, then research market, regulations, competitors and prices.

**Read as:** SME digitalisation grant discovery software · customer: Singapore SMEs · market: Singapore

## Key takeaways

This is an uncrowded niche with a weakened premise: the named competitors are nonprofit and grantmaker platforms rather than SME tools [^8][^11][^14][^16], but the EDG, MRA and PSG schemes the idea was built around ceased on 29 September 2026 and support now runs through the EDGE Grant [^6]. The regulatory load is light and mostly indirect, centred on PDPA obligations for handling SME data [^4][^5], and there is visible public support infrastructure through Enterprise Singapore and NCSS funding routes [^3]. Pricing room looks confined to the low end, since comparable tools start around $69/month and climb to $299–$999/month [^18][^19][^23], while the SME segment that would buy this is already 96.4% digitally adopted [^1]. The biggest opportunity is a Singapore-specific, low-cost EDGE Grant navigator with no direct local rival identified so far; the biggest risk is that the product's original hook has closed and demand must be rebuilt around a new scheme whose details are not yet in the evidence. The first thing to validate is how SMEs are actually finding and applying for EDGE Grant support after 30 September 2026, and whether they will pay for that discovery at a low-end price point.

## 1. Market size and growth

Singapore's digital economy is large and still growing faster than the wider economy: value-add reached S$144.1 billion in 2025, or 19.3% of GDP, up from S$136.5 billion and 18.8% in 2024, with 5.6% year-on-year growth against 3.1% nominal GDP growth [^1]. Adoption is also broad rather than niche, with 96.4% of enterprises adopting at least one of six digital areas in 2025, up from 95.1% in 2024 [^1]. For an SME digitalisation grant discovery tool, this suggests the underlying pool of digitally active SMEs is close to saturation, so the addressable market is less about converting non-adopters and more about serving firms already shopping for tools and funding. SME technology adoption rose from 74% in 2018 to 95% in 2023, with Enterprise Singapore supporting SME digitalisation through schemes such as the Productivity Solutions Grant and the Enterprise Development Grant [^2]. Public sources found so far do not give a dollar size for the grant-discovery software segment itself, which means the revenue ceiling for this specific product category is not yet quantified.

## 2. Policy and regulation

The regulatory load here is moderate and mostly indirect. The Personal Data Protection Act 2012 establishes a general data protection law governing collection, use and disclosure of personal data by organisations [^4], and Singapore-based CSPs must meet the Data Protection Provisions when processing personal data as data intermediaries [^5]. A grant discovery tool that stores SME profiles, financial details and application histories would sit squarely inside that regime, so data handling obligations are a real design constraint rather than an afterthought. On the funding side, organisations can apply to Enterprise Singapore for subsidy support for DPTM certification and consultancy services [^3], and social service agencies and charities can apply to the National Council of Social Service for funding support [^3]. Those routes point to adjacent funding channels, but public sources found so far do not describe a licensing or approval regime specific to grant-matching software, which means barriers to entry from regulation look low.

## 3. Government grants and support

This is the most important finding for the idea, and it cuts against the premise. Enterprise Singapore states that the EDG, MRA and PSG grants ceased on 29 September 2026 [^6], and from 30 September 2026 onwards business grant support is applied for under the EDGE Grant [^6]. So the founder's understanding that new EDG and PSG applications are still open does not match the official position as dated. The old schemes were substantial: the PSG offered up to 50% of eligible costs for local SMEs, with up to $30,000 [^7], and eligibility required at least 30% local equity and group annual sales turnover not exceeding S$100 million or group employment not exceeding 200 employees [^7]. That means the product's original hook, PSG and EDG discovery, is tied to schemes that have closed, and the near-term opportunity depends on how much of the EDGE Grant's structure and criteria mirror what SMEs already know.

## 4. Competitive landscape

The named competitors are all grant-management platforms, but they serve a different customer than Singapore SMEs. GrantBite is a Berlin-based AI funding platform for discovering, matching, applying for and managing grants, aimed at startups, nonprofits, researchers and individuals [^8][^9][^10]. Instrumentl is grant management SaaS for nonprofits, universities and consultants covering the full lifecycle in one platform [^11][^12][^13], Temelio is an all-in-one grants management and grants intelligence platform for foundations, universities and other grantmakers [^14][^15], and Grant Frog is grants management SaaS for nonprofits and grant professionals to find funders, track proposals, reports and deadlines [^16][^17]. The pattern is clear: these tools are built for grantseekers in the nonprofit and research world, or for grantmakers, not for SMEs navigating national business grant schemes. Public sources found so far do not identify a Singapore-specific SME grant discovery competitor, which means the local niche looks uncrowded, though it also means there is no proven local willingness to pay to point to.

## 5. Competitor pricing

Published pricing across the comparable tools sits well above what a small Singapore SME would likely pay for a discovery tool. GrantBite lists a Starter plan at $69/month excluding VAT, or $89/month with $69/month billed annually, with a Professional plan also listed but its price not shown [^18][^8]. Instrumentl publishes Discover at $299/month, Pre-Award at $499/month, Full Lifecycle at $999/month and Enterprise custom [^19][^20]. Grant Frog lists Professional at $199/month ($2,388 annually), Premium at $399/month ($4,788 annually), Elite at $799/month ($9,588 annually) and Custom [^23]. Temelio publishes no plan tiers, only a Foundation Sponsorship Program rate of $4,000 per year for one small foundation [^21][^22][^14]. The low end of this range, around $69/month, is the only price point that plausibly maps onto a Singapore SME budget, which suggests pricing room exists at the low end but not at the mid or high end of the observed market.

## What to validate next yourself

- Whether target customers really have this pain (needs customer interviews)
- Whether customers will pay your price (needs customer or transaction evidence)
- Check the specific eligibility criteria and application process for the EDGE Grant to ensure your software can accurately match SMEs.
- Assess the pricing and feature sets of existing grant platforms (e.g., GrantBite, Instrumentl) to identify differentiation opportunities.
- Investigate data protection requirements under the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) for handling SME data in your software.
