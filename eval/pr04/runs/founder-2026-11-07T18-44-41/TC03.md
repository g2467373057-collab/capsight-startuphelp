# TC03 · Pet grooming records: market research report

> Test run, In-vitro (fictional founder idea). Generated 2026-11-07T18:49:47.264Z. Evidence only; the founder decides.

**Founder prompt:** I want subscription software for independent pet grooming shops in Singapore to manage grooming records, training records and customer consent. Please research the market, applicable rules, grants, competitors and prices. Clearly say what you cannot verify.

**Read as:** Pet grooming shop management software · customer: Independent pet grooming shops · market: Singapore

## Key takeaways

This is a crowded market at the platform level, with four established international players already covering grooming, boarding and training workflows [^7][^9][^11][^14], but none of the evidence shows a Singapore-localised offer built around consent and training records. The regulatory load that can be verified is data protection under the PDPA, including obligations when personal data is brought into Singapore [^3][^4], which is a manageable but real compliance requirement rather than a sector-specific licensing burden. Support exists in the form of the EDGE Grant, which replaced the PSG from 30 September 2026 [^5][^6], though it is unconfirmed whether software subscriptions qualify. Pricing room appears to sit at the low end, since the cheapest visible tier is $29/month against incumbents mostly at $49–$199 [^17][^20][^22], and the biggest opportunity is a low-cost, PDPA-aligned tool for independent shops, while the biggest risk is that international platforms add localisation faster than a new entrant gains traction. The first thing to validate is whether independent Singapore shops will pay for consent and training-record features that the incumbents do not visibly advertise, and at what price point.

## 1. Market size and growth

The evidence available on market size is macro-level and does not describe the pet grooming sector itself. Singapore's services exports grew 2.3 per cent year-on-year in the fourth quarter of 2025 and 3.5 per cent for the full year, slowing from 13.2 per cent in 2024 [^1], while the accommodation sector grew 6.6 per cent in the fourth quarter and 3.1 per cent for the full year, easing from 5.0 per cent [^2]. These figures suggest a broadly expanding but decelerating services economy, which is at best a background signal for demand. Public sources found so far do not give a count of independent pet grooming shops in Singapore, their revenue, or software spend, so the actual addressable market for this product cannot be sized from the evidence provided.

## 2. Policy and regulation

The regulatory picture that can be verified is about data protection rather than grooming-specific licensing. The Personal Data Protection Act 2012 governs how organisations collect, use and disclose individuals' personal data [^3], and an organisation collecting personal data from outside Singapore for its own purposes must comply with all Data Protection Provisions from the time the data enters Singapore [^4]. For a product holding grooming records, training records and customer consent, this points to consent capture and data-handling compliance as core product requirements rather than optional features. The evidence does not cover any grooming-specific licensing, animal welfare or record-keeping rules, so whether shops face sector-specific obligations beyond PDPA cannot be confirmed here.

## 3. Government grants and support

The grant landscape has recently shifted in a way that matters for how shops might pay for software. The Productivity Solutions Grant ceased on 29 September 2026 [^5], and from 30 September onwards Singapore companies apply for business grant support under the EDGE Grant [^5], which covers 8 business areas and over 150 activities [^6]. For context, the PSG had offered up to 50% of eligible costs for local SMEs, with up to $30,000 [^5]. The evidence does not say whether software subscriptions such as this one fall within the EDGE Grant's covered activities, so it is not possible to confirm that a shop could offset the cost through the scheme.

## 4. Competitive landscape

The competitive field is already populated by established, multi-vertical pet-care platforms. MoeGo is an all-in-one pet care business software for grooming salons, mobile groomers, boarding, daycare and retail, covering booking, scheduling, payments and marketing [^7][^8]. Gingr serves boarding kennels, doggy daycare, grooming salons, training and dog park businesses with bookings, payments, PreCheck and report cards [^9][^10], while DaySmart Pet covers scheduling, reminders, payments, marketing and reporting for grooming shops, mobile groomers, kennels, daycare and boarding [^11][^12][^13]. Pawfinity is US-made cloud software for grooming salons, mobile grooming, kennels, daycare, training and sitting [^14][^15][^16]. All four are broad international platforms rather than Singapore-specific tools, and the evidence does not show any of them offering Singapore-localised consent or training-record handling, which is the gap this idea could occupy.

## 5. Competitor pricing

Published pricing shows the incumbents sit at mid-to-premium monthly price points. MoeGo's mobile grooming tiers run Basic $49, Growth $99, Ultimate $159 and Enterprise custom, with boarding/daycare tiers priced on demo [^17]. Gingr lists Spa at $109/month ($100 annual), Play at $169/month ($142 annual) and Stay at $179/month ($154 annual, including Integrated Payments), with Enterprise on contact [^18][^19]. DaySmart Pet runs Basic $29, Deluxe $69, Deluxe Growth $149 and Premium $199, with Premium Growth on request [^20][^21], and Pawfinity charges $100/month billed annually ($110 monthly) for Royal, $55/month annually ($60 monthly) for Groom & Train and the same for Stay & Play [^22][^14]. The lowest entry point visible is DaySmart's $29 Basic tier [^20], which suggests room for a lower-priced, Singapore-focused subscription, though the evidence does not show what each tier includes or how local demand responds to these prices.
